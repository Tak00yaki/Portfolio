# Assumptions log

Every modelling assumption, numbered so code and settings files can point to it (`[A8]`).
Status: **data** = fitted from race data · **paper** = taken from a cited paper ·
**rules** = from the FIA regulations (verify against the exact edition) ·
**heuristic** = a reasoned choice · **placeholder** = a guess that must be replaced or
sourced before results depend on it.

## Weeks 3–4: ETH-style simulator (`racesim.py`, `eras/*.toml`)

## Weeks 7–8: the exact optimiser (`7th_8th_week.py`)

| # | Assumption | Status | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| A31 | For a fixed pit plan the energy problem is convex: the fuel and battery terms (A16, A19) are convex, car mass and tyre wear are linear in fuel use, and every limit is linear. So IPOPT's optimum is that plan's global optimum, and the best over all plans is the exact optimum. Checked numerically: 10 random starting points per plan land on the same time (spread < 1e-10 s). IPOPT is run with `bound_relax_factor = 0` so no limit is exceeded even by its default 1e-8 relative slack. **If later weeks add a non-convex energy term (likely for 2026), this no longer holds** and each plan needs several starting points or a global solver. **Rechecked in Weeks 9–10** with the physics terms (deploy gain concave, clip and coast costs convex, all limits linear): 10 random starts on 5 plans each, under the FIA caps and with the harvest cap raised, spread ≤ 1e-10 s. | maths + numerical check | `RaceSim.energy_problem` |
| A32 | The search covers 1–2 stops (the brief), starting on softs (A24), every compound order, every stop lap, legal under A15: 9,620 plans. 3 stops is left out: every 3-stop plan with nominal energy is ≥ 10.7 s slower than the optimum, while optimising energy has saved at most 3.9 s on any plan, and the 50 best 3-stop plans re-solved with exact energy are all ≥ 7.3 s slower. The 1-stop plans exist but lose ≥ 12 s. | heuristic, checked | `7th_8th_week.py` steps 2–3 |
| A33 | The benchmark is for the model's mean driver with new sets at every stop (A21, A22). Real strategies are scored the same way, so "time lost" measures the plan only, not the driver, traffic or used sets. | heuristic | `7th_8th_week.py` step 4 |

## Weeks 9–10: energy terms from physics (`9th_10th_week.py`, `pointmass.py`)

Telemetry: OpenF1 `car_data` (speed, throttle, brake, DRS at ~4 Hz) for VER, PER, SAI and LEC, 2024 Bahrain, cut into laps with OpenF1 lap start times; clean laps as in A5 (203), plus 8 in-laps and 8 out-laps. FastF1 telemetry was blocked. The settings file selects the model (`energy.model = "physics"`); the Weeks 3–8 scripts pin `"placeholder"` so their logged numbers still reproduce.

| # | Assumption | Status | Where |
|---|---|---|---|
| A34 | Point-mass car on a flat track: wheel power 627 kW and drag area 1.75 m² fitted on 31,188 full-throttle samples (DRS closed, > 160 km/h; the four drivers within 2.8%). Rolling resistance 0.015 and air density 1.17 kg/m³ are fixed (not identifiable from the data). Braking follows each lap's measured profile from the throttle coming off, extended above the measured speed with the fitted straight-line deceleration 22.6 + 0.00072 v² m/s² (2.3–2.8 g). One constant force per straight, fitted to the measured braking-point speed, absorbs gradient, wind and the real car's deploy pattern. The result is 1.4% slow over the straights (90% of laps within 2.9%), because the real car accelerates harder early and de-rates at the end. Only full-throttle straights ≥ 2 s are used; part-throttle sections are ignored. | data / heuristic | `[pointmass]`, `pointmass.py` |
| A35 | Deploy gain: +120 kW (Art. 5.3.3) applied 25 m at a time where it saves most per MJ, starting from a car with no deploy (fitted power minus the nominal 2 MJ spread over the ~48 s on the throttle). Result: 1 MJ −0.78 s, 4 MJ −2.20 s (A19 guessed −0.83 s); 4.8 MJ fits on the straights of every lap, so the 4 MJ cap binds rather than the straights. Closed form 3.406·(1 − e^(−D/3.829)), within 0.007 s. Deploy and clip are separate terms: on the same lap they add up to within 1.4%. **Uncertainty: making every straight take exactly the measured time lowers the gain by 10% on average (17% at most).** That calibration is badly conditioned (+190 kW to remove 0.09 s), so it is used only as a check. | data / physics | `[energy.battery] deploy_*` |
| A36 | Free harvest: on every braking sample the MGU-K recovers min(120 kW, rear share × braking power), with braking power excluding drag and rolling resistance. The rear share is **0.4, a heuristic** (brake balance ~60% front); no electrical loss. Median 2.04 MJ/lap (10–90% 1.92–2.26), **reaching the FIA's 2 MJ cap on 65% of laps**. Rear share 0.5 gives 2.12 MJ, all braking power 2.30, a 5% loss 1.94. In-laps +0.29 MJ, out-laps +0.22 MJ (median over 8 each, against the driver's normal lap). | data / heuristic | `[energy.battery] free_harvest_mj`, `*_bonus_mj`, `[pointmass] rear_brake_share` |
| A37 | Extra harvest ("clipping"): −120 kW at full throttle where it costs least (the ends of the straights). Cost 0.014·X + 0.091·X² s (1 MJ +0.11 s, 2 MJ +0.39 s). The lap-time terms don't depend on car mass: the 4 MJ deploy gain changes by −4.3% from a full to an empty tank, and including it would couple fuel and battery and break convexity. They also don't depend on the lap, although in- and out-laps miss part of a straight in the pit lane. | data / physics; heuristic (no mass, no lap effect) | `[energy.battery] clip_*` |
| A38 | Fuel: using less than nominal means lifting and coasting before braking (no power on the stretches where it costs least), saving fuel at the full-flow rate (100 kg/h, Art. 5.2.3, × A12). Cost 0.00082·F^2.573 s, with F counted from no lifting at all; saving 10% (8.3 MJ) costs ~0.2 s (A16 guessed 0.47 s). The nominal lap already lifts 0.43 s/lap (median 0), worth 0.51 MJ, so the most a lap can use is nominal + 0.51 MJ (100.6%). That replaces the top of ETH's 90–110% band and follows from A11 (start fuel = what a flat-out race burns). | data / physics | `[energy.fuel] coast_*`, `lift_nominal_mj` |
| A39 | The battery decision is still one net number per lap (agent and environment unchanged). Inside the lap it is split as well as possible into deploy D, free harvest h and extra harvest X: all free harvest is taken unless the deploy cap prevents it, then X is raised while a MJ deployed gains more than it costs to clip. The simulator does this by bisection; the exact optimiser has D, h and X as variables, and the two agree to 1.6e-9 s. State of charge is only checked at the end of each lap. | heuristic | `RaceSim.split`, `RaceSim._physics_problem` |
| A40 | The battery term is zero at net-zero use on a normal lap under the real caps, the conditions the base lap was measured in. Sensitivity runs that change a cap keep that zero point. | heuristic | `RaceSim.b_ref`, `9th_10th_week.py` step 6 |
| A41 | 2026 preview only: the same 2024 car with a 350 kW MGU-K recovers 4.1 MJ/lap from braking, and deploying 8 MJ saves 4.6 s. No other 2026 rule (mass, aero, harvest cap, power taper) is applied, and nothing uses these numbers yet. | physics (preview) | `9th_10th_week.py` steps 2–3 |
